Narrator: Either Whole or Hole. By Chris Abraham.
Narrator: Men are only men when we're whole.
Narrator: The sentence arrived at a table, in conversation. A friend and I were comparing notes on our successes and failures, reading our own ledgers aloud the way men do when the hour gets honest, and the taxonomy surfaced from the accounting. It was heard before it was argued. Manhood, in the ledger, is a standing, and a standing can be lost. A male animal keeps his sex whatever happens to him. His standing as a man rides on entries other animals can read, entries he reads himself every morning, entries that balance or fail in public.
Narrator: The ledger
Narrator: The ledger is short. Every man can recite his own version of it in one breath, and the entries rhyme across every habitat I have ever watched. Whole: confident. Making money. Providing. Leading his family. Respected. The respect is the receipt for the other entries, the troop's signature at the bottom of the page. Hole: poor. Unemployed. Disrespected. Humiliated. The list trails off into an etcetera every man completes privately, from his own field notes, in his own hand.
Narrator: Wholeness is enacted. A man performs the entries daily or the entries lapse. Providing happens at a job, on a shift, in a transfer that lands. Leading happens at a table where people look up when he speaks and settle when he decides. Respect happens in rooms he did not arrange, among animals who owe him nothing and grant the standing because the record supports it. Regalia can be rented for an evening. The ledger gets audited over seasons.
Narrator: The hole is the same animal with entries missing. The notebook records the morph without heat. He wakes in the same body, carries the same history, holds the same name. The entries have stopped balancing, and every observer in the clearing can see the new balance, including the animal himself. He reads it first. He usually reads it hardest.
Narrator: The consolation, examined
Narrator: The culture keeps a consolation ready for the man in the hole, and the consolation deserves a careful entry of its own. The consolation redefines the terms. Whole, it says, names an intrinsic worth that survives setbacks. What a man has and who a man is are separate accounts, and the second account never overdraws. The prescription follows: separate the accounts, hold the second one steady, and the standing will follow the belief.
Narrator: Watch the troop grade the paper. The troop grades the enacted ledger. Employers grade the work history. Creditors grade the payments. A family grades the providing it can see at the table and the leading it can feel in a crisis. Strangers grade the signals in seconds and move on. Intrinsic worth appears on no grading sheet in the clearing. Men know this, because men do the grading, on each other and on themselves, every day. They believe the signals because the signals are the currency they themselves accept. The consolation circulates as literature. The ledger circulates as law.
Narrator: What the record shows
Narrator: The record at the bottom of the hole is written in mortality tables, and the tables support the taxonomy with terrible plainness. The federal count for 2024 puts the male suicide rate at about three to four times the female rate, a ratio the same count has carried for a decade. Women attempt more. Men die more. The lethality gap is part of the ledger's final page.
Narrator: Unemployment sits inside that gap. Unemployed people die by suicide at two to three times the rate of people in work. Samaritans reported that range in its study of disadvantaged men in mid-life, and a New Zealand cohort that linked two million adults in the census to three years of mortality records found the same range for unemployed men. Long-term poverty presses harder than a single job loss in the studies that separate the two. The entries compound. A man can lose the providing entry, then the respect entry, then the belonging entry, in sequence, each loss making the next one easier for the troop to record and harder for the animal to reverse.
Narrator: The clinicians keep their own vocabulary for the same columns. Defeat. Humiliation. Entrapment, the state the literature calls arrested flight: the animal has run, every door has shut, and the running itself has stopped. Perceived burdensomeness, a man's settled belief that the troop eats better without him. Thwarted belongingness, the bond entry at zero for a long season. Translate the terms back into the ledger and they match, entry for entry. The clinic and the table arrived at the same accounting by different trails through the same habitat.
Narrator: Willard between missions
Narrator: The cinema's cleanest specimen of the hole is a soldier in a hotel room. Captain Willard opens Apocalypse Now in Saigon, between missions, drinking through the ceiling fan's rotation, punching a mirror, bleeding, weeping, alone with a record that has stopped. His own narration gives the diagnosis in one line: he is still only in Saigon. Every day he spends in that room, he gets weaker. The hole, in Willard's case, has a precise shape. He has no assignment, no function. The institution that grades him has gone silent, and a man built for missions sits in the silence degrading like an unmaintained machine.
Narrator: The mission arrives, and the animal reassembles. The assignment is terrible. The orders send him upriver to end another man's command, a colonel the institution itself produced and then disowned. Willard takes the mission and becomes legible to himself again: function restored, standing restored, the ledger balancing on the strength of an errand soaked in horror. The film refuses comfort here, and the notebook follows the film. Terrible things happen to men in the hole, and terrible things are done by them. The hole is a dangerous habitat for the animal inside it and for every animal near it. A mission can fill it. The filling says nothing about the mission's worth. It says everything about the shape of the emptiness.
Narrator: The game that grades upkeep
Narrator: The biggest cultural artifact of this decade shipped the same week the taxonomy got its name, and the artifact grades its man on the ledger, entry by entry, in meters the player must maintain. Grand Theft Auto VI gives Jason Duval three core stats, Health, Stamina, and Focus, and it buffs them the way a body actually answers: food, sleep, a shower, a workout. Protein after training shortens the recovery. Skipped upkeep shows up as decayed performance. The game teaches a hundred million players that a man is a set of maintained meters, and that the meters run down on their own schedule whether the player attends to them or not.
Narrator: The bond gets a meter too. Jason's relationship with Lucia Caminos carries four levels, and keeping the rating full takes attention: time together, the small provisions, a stocked fridge, the couple kept in good order. Rockstar removed sex workers and cheating from the couple's story outright and told Shacknews the game is about a couple in a bind, lovers on the run. The outlaws are monogamous, and the game grades the monogamy.
Narrator: The money gets the strictest meter of all. Early in the story the couple opens a BuckMe account, and its Family Savings side accepts deposits a player can never withdraw. The milestones climb from $5,000 to $1 million, five goals in all, and each tier raises the checking limit and unlocks discounts, as if the game itself were paying interest on domestic discipline. Crime supplies the income line. Everything the game actually rewards is the domestic ledger: saving toward family goals, toward retirement, toward a household that holds.
Narrator: The house completes the account. Before Lucia comes home, Jason repairs the place with his hands: the front steps, the leaky roof, the railing, an interior wall. Body, bond, bank, building. The ledger the table named, rendered as gameplay. Even the game's eye works from the habitat: the women it presents as desirable are petite, thick-hipped, hoop-earringed, built like the actual population of the place rather than a casting call. The lowest review on the board scored the game 80 and complained, in terms, that the upkeep is a chore. The complaint confirms the design. Maintenance reads as a chore to a culture that expected the standing to be permanent. The game knows better. The standing is the upkeep, and the upkeep is daily, and a crime empire turns out to be the easy part.
Narrator: The patience owed to both morphs
Narrator: A naturalist who has watched a troop for a long season learns to give the fallen animal the same quality of attention as the standing one. The hole morph shows the species under load. His behavior in the hole, the pacing, the withdrawal, the sudden rages at small provocations, the long sleeps at wrong hours, reads clearly once the ledger is understood as the grading sheet he himself uses. Contempt adds nothing to the record. The record is heavy enough.
Narrator: The way back, where a way back exists, runs through the same ledger in small entries. A shift worked. A bill paid on the day it was due. A roof patched, a sink repaired, a table where somebody looks up when he speaks. Standing returns the way it left, entry by entry, in public, over seasons. The culture's biggest game now teaches exactly that arithmetic for hours a day. The table's taxonomy and the game's meters agree because both were taken from the same habitat, observed closely, by watchers who recorded what the animals actually grade.
