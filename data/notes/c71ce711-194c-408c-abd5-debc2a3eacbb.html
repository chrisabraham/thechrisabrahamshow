More on This Episode

This is the middle of three episodes that started with Malcolm Gladwell on The Oprah Podcast, "Why Is America Different Than Every Other Country When It Comes to Guns?", about his book The American Way of Killing: The Invention of an Epidemic. S11E1 reacted to the first half. The last stretch of this episode reacts to the second half, which I think narrows almost entirely to inner-city violence and leaves out concealed carry states, home defense, and white neighborhoods. S11E3: Broken Windows, Missing Wheels picks the thread back up with theft, crime statistics, and The Tipping Point.

Glossary

Russian roulette, as a metaphor
In the game, one round goes into a six-shot revolver and the odds are one in six on every pull. My version: each risky behavior in a confrontation (getting in someone's face, ignoring orders, closing distance, touching) is another round in the cylinder. The other person's fear, training, and history decide when it fires, not your intentions. More.
Escalation ladder
The steps by which a confrontation climbs from words to force to deadly force, each side answering the other one rung up. Police are trained to escalate with you, not to absorb it.
"The First Amendment is not a force field against the Second Amendment"
Having the right to speak or protest doesn't make you physically safe in front of armed people. Rights describe what the law protects afterward, not what happens in the moment.
Trust fall
The team-building exercise where you fall backward and trust someone to catch you. Here, the expectation that strangers, police, or the world in general will catch you. Nobody owes you one.
Musturbation
A term from the psychologist Albert Ellis, founder of rational emotive behavior therapy, for turning preferences into demands: because something should happen, it must happen, and it's unbearable when it doesn't. Ellis is usually credited with the related line I reach for at the end, about not "shoulding" all over yourself.
"Guard your heart"
Proverbs 4:23: "Above all else, guard your heart, for everything you do flows from it" (NIV). Not Psalms, as Siri reminds me.
Aphantasia and SDAM
Aphantasia is having no mind's eye. Severely deficient autobiographical memory (SDAM) is a weak memory for one's own past: I know things happened without being able to relive them. Both make it hard to imagine how long other people hold a grudge. More.
Panopticon
Jeremy Bentham's design for a prison where every cell can be watched from one tower. Now shorthand for cameras everywhere, which is why protest isn't anonymous or forgotten anymore. More.
Cesar Chavez and undocumented labor
The farmworker leader's United Farm Workers opposed the use of undocumented workers as strikebreakers in the 1960s and '70s, because cheap replacement labor broke strikes and held wages down. I use it to argue that immigration enforcement can be about fairness for workers, not only about race. More.
Salad bar Catholic
Also "cafeteria Catholic": someone who takes the parts of a creed they like and skips the rest. I apply it to Trump's Republicanism.
Neocon and neolib
Neoconservatives favor an interventionist foreign policy; neoliberals favor free trade and markets. I count myself against both, which is how I explain my votes.
Kent State
On May 4, 1970, Ohio National Guard troops fired on students protesting the Vietnam War at Kent State University, killing four and wounding nine. More.
"Lies, damned lies, and statistics"
Mark Twain made the line famous and credited it to Benjamin Disraeli. It's what I reach for after deciding Gladwell's comparison of Berlin's police force with Los Angeles' was cherry-picked. More.
