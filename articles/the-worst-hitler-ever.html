The Worst Hitler Ever

An article by Chris Abraham, November 7, 2026

This article, read aloud:

Share: X · Facebook · LinkedIn · Reddit · Bluesky · Threads · Email

People compare Donald Trump to the Adolf Hitler of the 1930s. I accept the comparison long enough to grade it. Donald Trump is the worst Hitler ever.

He can't Hitler. He can't fascist. He is all talk, all pretend, all voice, all drawn that way. He just looks like a villain. His villainy isn't jackbooting enough.

Objective Daily News has published the cold file under this argument: “The Word and the Record,” Special Report No. 3. The report prints the scholars in their own frameworks, the court record in both directions, the government's own ICE death list, the deportation record across administrations, and the state of the institutions after the November 3 midterms. It gives the reader the record and stops there. This essay is mine. It takes the same record and renders the judgment the paper leaves unrendered.

Trump works by performance. He holds rallies, invents nicknames, starts feuds, issues threats, signs orders, dares courts to stop him, and treats every camera as a room he has just entered. Performance can injure people. Performance can corrupt a party and degrade a government. A Hitler performance still needs the machinery of Hitlerism behind it: a party militia answering to the leader, rival parties driven from public life, unions smashed as independent powers, courts reduced to clerks, newspapers closed or seized, elections converted into rituals, and murder organized by the state. Trump has spent a decade producing the sound of that machinery. The machinery itself keeps failing to appear.

Slacker Hitler

Trump is slacker Hitler, and he is not even Gen X. He was born in 1946, at the front edge of the Baby Boom. The slacker part is a borrowed costume from a younger generation: grievance as identity, irony as armor, the permanent refusal to do the reading, the insistence that the pose can substitute for the project. Hitler wrote the project, built the party, built the terror apparatus, and dragged Europe into the abyss. Trump builds a day of programming and waits for the ratings.

The visual work is superb. The glare. The hair. The suit, the long tie, the raised chin, the enemies named into a microphone. He enters politics already lit and costumed as the heavy. Central casting could retire on him. Under the costume sits a familiar American figure: a celebrity landlord, a tabloid creature, a television host with a genius for keeping his name in the crawl. He wants the crown. He has shown very little interest in building the dungeon.

That distinction matters because the word “Hitler” carries a specific historical weight. It names a regime that abolished democratic government and industrialized murder. Used as a synonym for a president we hate, it stops measuring anything. It becomes a noise the audience makes when the villain walks onstage.

Fascist since 1946?

Here is the question the current panic keeps avoiding: if Donald Trump is literally a fascist, is Democrat America any less fascist than Republican America?

If America is fascist, America has been fascist since 1946. The postwar republic built a permanent military and intelligence establishment, a nuclear arsenal, a worldwide archipelago of bases, covert-action capacity, emergency powers renewed year after year, and a presidency equipped to make war without ever again asking Congress for a declaration. Republicans and Democrats have taken turns operating that state. Each party discovers its dangers mainly while the other party holds the keys.

The post-9/11 settlement makes the continuity plain. Presidents of both parties have renewed the emergency declaration first issued on September 14, 2001. Congresses of both parties have sustained mass surveillance authorities. The security bureaucracy grew under George W. Bush, kept its powers under Barack Obama, served Donald Trump, served Joe Biden, and serves Trump again. A theory of American fascism that begins with Trump's face has mistaken a change in casting for the creation of the theater.

Immigration enforcement tells the same story in the Department of Homeland Security's own numbers. The figures are event counts, removals and returns combined on the broadest measure. Barack Obama's eight years produced 5,248,893. Joe Biden's four years produced about 4,786,000. Donald Trump's first term produced about 2,002,000 on the same broad construction. His second administration stood at about 980,000 through July 31, 2026, with detention and deaths in custody at record levels. Those current records deserve their own judgment. The raw removal totals still belong to Democratic administrations.

Obama earned the name “Deporter-in-Chief” from Janet Murguía of the National Council of La Raza in 2014. That was before Trump descended the escalator. The machine was already built, already staffed, already deporting at a scale Trump has yet to match on the administration totals. Trump made the performance crueler and louder. He inherited the machinery from presidents the country now remembers as the reasonable alternative.

The charge is a century old

Hippies and lefties and progressives and American communists and American socialists and teenagers and people in college have been calling America fascist since 1946. The line began earlier. Labor organizers, communists, socialists, and antiwar radicals used the word against American employers, police, courts, and presidents during the 1920s, 1930s, and 1940s. Every generation since has refreshed the charge and aimed it at the government in front of it.

The word itself dates to 1919. The accusation predates the word. During the robber-baron age, its names were plutocrat, money power, trust, tyranny, and imperialist. Later came militaristic thug, oppressive overlord, crusader, capitalist pig, and capitalist swine. O. Henry coined “banana republic” in 1904, drawing on Honduras and the fruit companies. Americans were describing corporate power reaching into weaker countries and bending their governments long before Mussolini supplied the vocabulary that stuck.

The historical exhibits are familiar. Operation Paperclip brought German scientists, including men who had served the Nazi war machine, into American rocket and weapons programs because Washington wanted their knowledge in the Cold War. The United States kept its boot on Cuba through embargo, invasion by proxy, and decades of pressure. It fought a catastrophic war in sovereign Vietnam. It backed anti-communist governments whose prisons and death squads were open secrets. People called all of that fascist at the time. They called the presidents who ran it fascist. Most of those presidents were Democrats or respectable bipartisan statesmen of the Cold War center.

Trump arrived after eighty years of this indictment. His opponents present the word as an emergency discovery, a diagnosis made necessary by one unprecedented man. The record shows a standing American argument. The country has been accused of fascism through world war, Cold War, Vietnam, Watergate, the War on Terror, Occupy, and the smartphone age. A fascist by any other name is just as swine.

The casting test

Barack Obama supplies the control case. There is no way Obama could be a fascist because he looked beautiful and wore a tan suit. And he had a beautiful black queen as a wife.

That judgment tells us how the label actually moves through American public life. Obama deported millions on the broad event count. He preserved the surveillance state, used drone warfare as a routine instrument, protected the architecture of the War on Terror, and governed through the same executive machinery now described as fascist when Trump touches it. His elegance gave those facts a different moral weather. The suit made the power look administrative. The family made the presidency look like a magazine cover. The voice made hard policy sound like a seminar.

Here is a musing, punctuation and all: Barack Hussein Obama probably spent more of his adult life as an asset rather than as an academic. Probably, comma, allegedly, period.

Trump reverses every visual cue. He scowls. He boasts. He paints himself orange, piles up gold, speaks in threats, and performs domination as entertainment. His opponents see the costume and complete the historical analogy from the silhouette. Trump is the only president who has ever been the perfect cartoon villain. The novelty lives in the casting.

American politics now grades power by whether the audience can stand looking at the person exercising it. A beautiful president can run the machine and receive absolution for the machinery. An ugly performance at the same controls triggers the Hitler comparison. That is a theory of aesthetics dressed as a theory of regimes.

The floor under the comparison

The factual floor matters because “worst Hitler ever” should work as a measurement, with the joke carried inside the measurement. Nazi Germany destroyed elections as a real choice, outlawed or crushed opposition parties, abolished independent unions, subordinated the courts, built a police state, murdered political opponents, and carried out the industrialized murder of approximately six million Jews and millions of other people. It launched a world war that killed tens of millions. Fascist Italy destroyed parliamentary government, murdered the socialist deputy Giacomo Matteotti, enacted racial laws, built a one-party dictatorship, and pursued wars of conquest, including in Ethiopia.

That is the reference class. Those regimes converted power into a closed system at speed. The leader's party became the state. Independent centers of power were eliminated. Violence moved from rhetoric and episodic abuse into the organizing principle of government.

The United States in November 2026 gives us a different institutional picture. Americans voted in the midterm elections on November 3. The opposition won both chambers of Congress. Opposition politicians are preparing investigations and speaking freely about retribution. Newspapers and networks attack the president every day and remain open. Courts hear challenges to his administration by the hundreds. Judges have ruled against him, sometimes in language of open contempt for his agencies' conduct, and those fights continue in public. None of that makes the administration gentle or lawful in every particular. It does place the country far from the abolition list that defines the reference regimes.

The scholars themselves show why the cold file had to come first. Robert O. Paxton applies the fascist label to Trump after January 6. Roger Griffin declines to apply it. Stanley G. Payne declines to apply it. Jason Stanley applies his ten pillars of fascist politics. Serious people can fight over trajectory, rhetoric, and political style. The completed-regime comparison still fails on the visible institutional facts: no sourced element of the 2025–26 American record reaches the category or scale of Nazi Germany or Fascist Italy.

Trump's second administration has produced real abuses and real suffering, and the ODN file prints them without softening them: deaths in ICE custody at a record fiscal-year level on the government's own list, lower-court findings of violated orders, aggressive third-country removals, and a detention system under extraordinary strain. Those facts warrant opposition, litigation, journalism, and electoral punishment. They also deserve accurate names. Stretching Hitler over them hides their actual shape.

Worst Hitler ever

Donald Trump deserves judgment as an American president, under American measures: his words, his orders, his appointments, his corruption, his cruelty, his respect for elections when he loses, and his willingness to use federal power against enemies. Voters have plenty to judge. They judged him again on November 3, and his party lost Congress. That is how this system registers a verdict while the system remains standing.

The Hitler comparison skips the American judgment and imports a European catastrophe for scale. It flatters the speaker's alarm and flatters Trump at the same time. It gives a television performer the stature of the twentieth century's central murderer, then discovers that he cannot carry the role. He talks like a strongman, poses like a strongman, and governs through noise, litigation, personnel chaos, and the next episode.

So I will keep the comparison and return the grade. Trump is the worst fascist ever because he is the worst Hitler ever: slacker Hitler, Boomer Hitler, Hitler as content. The villain costume fits him perfectly. The jackboots remain too big.
