History Goes to the Cowards

An article by Chris Abraham, November 1, 2026

Share: X · Facebook · LinkedIn · Reddit · Bluesky · Threads · Email

Every person alive descends, in overwhelming proportion, from the people of their generations who stayed alive. Not the bravest. Not the most glorious. The ones still breathing when the breeding happened. History keeps two sets of books on this. The story ledger records names, monuments, last words, and it overrepresents heroes wildly. The DNA ledger records descendants, and it underrepresents them just as wildly. Both ledgers are honest. They account for different things, and confusing them is the founding error of heroic history: we mistake being remembered for being ancestral. Weighted by actual descendants, the median ancestor of everyone now living is an unknown who kept his head down through somebody else's war and raised his children in a village nobody chronicled.

The troop keeps all three kinds

Start where the labels fall away. Among side-blotched lizards, males come in three hereditary strategies that cycle like rock-paper-scissors: aggressive orange-throats hold harems and take females from everyone; blue-throats guard one or two mates closely and fend off thieves; yellow-throats, colored like females, defend nothing and sneak copulations on the oranges' own ground. Sinervo and Lively published the game in Nature in 1996, and the cycle has been running for some fifteen million years. No strategy wins. No strategy disappears. The sneaker is not a defect in the population; he is one of its three permanent load-bearing members.

Biologists call these alternative reproductive tactics, and they are everywhere once you look. Bluegill sunfish grow up into territorial nest-holders or into small sneakers who dart in and fertilize eggs over the big male's shoulder. Young coho salmon return early as jacks and steal spawnings from the hooknosed fighters. Chimpanzee subordinates arrange consortships away from the alpha's eye; baboon males build patient "friendships" with females that pay out in paternity the troop's tyrant never audits. The literature's word for all of this is tactic. The barroom word is coward. They name the same animal, and the animal is in every lineage that has ever lasted, because a population of pure fighters is a population one bad season from extinction.

War is a filter, and it filters backward

In 1902 the founding president of Stanford, David Starr Jordan, published The Blood of the Nation (full text), arguing that war reverses selection: it takes the tallest, strongest, and most courageous young men to be killed, and leaves the rejected, the exempt, and the evasive at home to father the next generation. Jordan was a eugenicist, and the remedies his movement built—sterilization laws, immigration bars—were monstrous and are part of this record. His observation is separable from his program, and the observation has never been answered: conscription is a sieve whose mesh is woven from exactly the traits the recruiting poster celebrates.

Run the sieve over the twentieth century and read what fell through. France buried roughly 1.4 million soldiers in the First World War. Britain's 1921 census counted 19,803,022 women against 18,082,220 men in England and Wales, and the newspapers named the difference the surplus two million—women for whom the marriage market no longer contained a husband. The Second World War scaled it. In the Soviet Union, by the figures Elizabeth Brainerd drew from the archives, the ratio of men to women among twenty-somethings fell from .91 in 1941 to .65 in 1946; across the whole country women outnumbered men by some twenty million. Two of every three German men born in 1918 did not survive Hitler's war. In the Berlin suburb of Treptow in February 1946, among adults aged nineteen to twenty-one, there were 181 men for 1,105 women.

Add the detail the monuments leave off. The heroic dead were seventeen, eighteen, nineteen. Whatever children a hero had were conceived in a year or two of leave and letters, and then his line's breeding stopped at the age when most men's begins. The widows were seventeen, eighteen, nineteen too, with forty fertile years in front of them, and those years did not stay empty. Brainerd's Russian data show the affected cohorts marrying less, divorcing more, and bearing more children outside marriage—which is the statistical shadow of the men who remained fathering across the gap. Every subsequent child of a war widow had a father who, by definition, had not died a hero. The filter does not argue. It just keeps the books.

America is the control case, and it proves the rule by weakening it. The United States lost some 400,000 men, a fraction of the European and Soviet toll, and its Baby Boom was fathered in large part by the sixteen million who served and came home to the GI Bill's marriage market. Where the catastrophe was smaller, the heroes' share of the next generation stayed larger. The coward's dividend scales with the disaster. It is not a moral quantity at all. It is a ratio.

The dividend

State the mechanism plainly, because it is old and documented and needs no plot. When a generation's most competitive young men are removed, the men who remain—second choices in an intact market—become first choices in the depleted one. Their competition is buried in Flanders and Stalingrad. The internet's manosphere tells this as a deliberate conspiracy, a beta revolt arranged by elites, and that part is folklore. The demographic machinery underneath the folklore is Jordan's, and it runs whether anyone arranges it or not. A wink belongs here, and it will be labeled as one: run the logic forward onto the present war in the Donbas, where two countries are feeding their most willing men into the same ground season after season, and a reader can feel the speculation assemble itself—hero-depleted nations, safety prized over freedom, a continent of the compliant. The record supports the sieve. It does not support the plot. The sieve is enough.

Every road runs through a nursery

The strongest objection is kin selection, and it is real. W. D. Hamilton showed that a hero who dies saving his siblings transmits his genes through them; Richard Dawkins built The Selfish Gene on the arithmetic. Follow it one step further and it surrenders. The saved siblings carry the hero's genes into the future by staying alive, marrying, and raising children—including, very often, his. His sacrifice reaches the present riding in a survivor's household or it does not reach the present at all. There is no other road. The nursery is the only port every lineage in history has ever shipped through.

Sexual selection files the same confession. Bravery is status, status is mating access, and heroes did out-reproduce cautious men—when they came home. Costly signaling only pays a survivor; the medal buys mates for the living man wearing it. The dead hero's prestige accrues to his family's account, his brothers' prospects, his children's rations, and is spent by the living on the living. Even glory's wages are collected at a survivor's window.

The quiet substitutions

The sieve has a quieter mesh, woven inside marriage itself. The literature calls it paternal discrepancy: a child raised by a man who is not the biological father. Bellis and his co-authors reviewed the world's studies in 2005 and found rates from 0.8 to 30 percent with a median of 3.7. The spread is the story. Kermyt Anderson's comparative work separates it by the man's own confidence: where confidence in paternity is high, measured nonpaternity runs near 2 percent; where a man already doubts enough to seek a test, near 30. And across deep time, small numbers compound. Larmuseau's team matched Y-chromosomes to four centuries of Flemish genealogies and found extra-pair paternity of 1 to 2 percent per generation, stable since the late Middle Ages. Do the arithmetic the archives invite: at 1.5 percent a generation, roughly one patriline in four carries a quiet substitution within twenty generations. Nearly every old surname on earth is, somewhere along its length, a social fact wearing a biological costume.

Institutions know this, and their behavior is the confession. The common law answered with the presumption of legitimacy: a child born to a married woman is her husband's, full stop, and the question is made obscene so it cannot be asked at scale. France carries the logic to its end—private paternity testing is illegal outside a court order, punishable by a year in prison and a €15,000 fine, and the French Council of State defends the ban as preserving the peace of families. The Republic looked at the assay and chose the story. Every legal system on earth has made some version of the same choice, because a civilization that audited every cradle would be auditing the foundation under its own house.

The modern rumor inverts the numbers and completes the damage. Online, the clinic's 30 percent—the rate among men who already doubted—circulates as the rate for everyone, a copy with no original, and men price it on arrival. The truth, 2 percent among the confident, would be absorbable news. The rumor is a suspicion tax on marrying and breeding at all, collected from exactly the men whose trust was thinnest, and it suppresses more families than the substitutions ever did. I have written elsewhere about a dream that runs on disincentive. The rumor is one of the disincentives, wearing a lab coat.

The receipt

Coward is a story-ledger word. In the DNA ledger the same trait wears its working names: prudence, endurance, the deserter who lived to be a grandfather, the 4-F whose grandchildren run banks, the second choice who became the only choice and rose to it. None of this indicts the heroes. It prices the monuments correctly. A monument is a payment in the only currency the dead can hold, issued by the living who kept the change in descendants. We know the heroes' names. We descend from the cowards' loins. Both sentences are true, and the second one is the census.

The author files no exemption for himself. He has written about his own professional cowardice in this same spirit, as bookkeeping rather than shame. Every reader of this page is the descendant of thousands who ran, hid, ducked, deserted, feigned, failed the physical, married the widow, or raised another man's child as his own—and of a thin, bright garnish of the ones who stood. History goes to the cowards because history, in the end, is written by the living, and the living are the cowards' posterity. The heroes got the glory. The cowards got the future. Read the two ledgers side by side and ask which one you are standing in.
