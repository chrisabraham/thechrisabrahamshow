Here's Your Warning

An article by Chris Abraham, October 26, 2026

This article, read aloud:

Share: X · Facebook · LinkedIn · Reddit · Bluesky · Threads · Email

Ahead of the midterms, HBO announced, John Oliver would devote an entire episode to the rising prominence of extremist beliefs within the Republican Party. I posted my review before I ever watched a minute of it: this story might have worked last year but—Nazi please! Then I watched it myself, because I believe in checking—as I write this, I am twenty-five minutes and nineteen seconds into a thirty-six-minute show, most of the way through it, judging the thing itself and not clips, fragments, or hearsay—and here is my considered verdict on the segment itself. What Oliver thinks he did is quarantine: drag the crazies into the light, read their wildest lines aloud, let the audience laugh, and send everybody home inoculated. What he actually did is platforming, and platforming is not quarantine; platforming is distribution. A segment like his is a de facto guarantee of popularity for the one man in it who matters. You cannot cartoonize a man who refuses to be cartoonized, and Nick Fuentes refuses. A year ago, that act might have worked. Today, the same show functions as promotion of the very extremism it mocks; the audience has moved out from underneath it, and the show never noticed.

Here is the mechanism, because a warning without a mechanism is just a mood. The segment names Nick Fuentes and the rest of the extremist talkers—streamers, podcasters, the whole circuit—by name, on HBO, in front of a large audience most of whom had never heard of any of them. Those people will now go and check them out; that is what audiences do with names they have just learned. Some fraction of them will be wooed—by the fluency, by the charm, by the discipline—people who would never have been exposed at all if a comedian in a suit had not introduced them. As a warning, this segment would have been a good idea eighteen months or two years ago. In October of 2026 it is promotion of the very thing it castigates, and its host is, in the classic phrase and in my considered judgment, a useful idiot. He believes the framing is the vaccine: the dead virus, administered with a laugh track, producing immunity. But inoculation requires a dead virus, and not all dead virus is dead. He is broadcasting the live man. It's less like inoculation and more like a measles party—deliberate exposure to the live thing, arranged by people who believe they are helping.

And here is the question Oliver never asks, the one I posted that same morning because it is the whole ballgame: why doesn't he talk about how extreme right-wing popularity is a direct response to extreme left-wing popularity, and that they feed off of each other? Every outrage on one pole is fundraising, recruitment, and permission for the other; the two extremes are not opponents so much as dance partners, each keeping the other in business. And both sides, strangely and tellingly, come out anti-Zionist and anti-semitic when you follow them far enough. Oliver treats one pole as the story and never touches the feedback loop between the poles, because the loop would implicate his own audience, his own side's fever, and his own show's business model—which is, not incidentally, the loop's favorite fuel. And the poles share more now than they used to, because both of them have become the same substance: populism, built on a populist base. Do the numbers the way I have started doing them. Twenty percent on one side believes what it believes, and twenty percent on the other side believes the opposite—and no matter what either twenty believes, it is the sixty percent in the middle who have all the momentum now, and they are all populists. All of them, and yes, "all" is hyperbole; I know an exaggeration when I make one. But the direction underneath the exaggeration is not hyperbole at all: the motion is in the middle, the middle has gone populist, and Oliver is up there mocking the fringe on behalf of an audience that no longer sits where he thinks it sits.

Worse, listen to what the mockery actually traffics in. Oliver read aloud, in a tone of high comedy, the craziest things his featured extremists were saying about Jewish people, and the studio audience laughed on cue, and that laughter is supposed to be the end of it. Friends, that laughter is a starting gun. A segment like that works as a kind of Olly Olly Oxen Free: it announces to a frightened country that the old explanation can be said out loud again, on television, with production values. America and the entire world are hunting for scapegoats; that is a media fact and a political fact, not anybody's brief. And I will add my own witness, for whatever fifty-six years of watching is worth: antisemitism is low-key higher right now than I have ever seen it in my life, and people are ripe for the scapegoat—on the left and on the right, in roughly equal readiness. And the scapegoat caricature that is always ready, always fitted, always the perfect hand in glove for this kind of movement is the oldest one in the drawer: the Elders of Zion caricature, the propaganda caricature of Zionism and Judaism as a hidden hand on the world. I will name it plainly, as analysis and as verdict: it is a caricature, it is propaganda, it was a forgery when the tsar's police cooked it up, and its durability has nothing to do with its truth and everything to do with how badly frightened people want one enemy with one face. And do not ask me to sort the categories neatly, because from where I sit they refuse to separate: the Free Palestine movement declares its support for Jews while being wholly anti-Zionist; plenty of secular, American Jews are anti-Zionist themselves; and the extreme right contains an entire Christian Zionist current that may not like Jews or Judaism one bit and would still do anything for the State of Israel, because it is the Holy Land and end-times prophecy requires it. Antisemitism, anti-Zionism, pro-Palestine, river to the sea—out of that whole tangle, every disclaimer of "I'm not antisemitic, I'm just anti-Zionist" lands on me the same way: it smells to me like the smoke out of the death ovens at Treblinka and the other death camps of the Holocaust.

So here is my confession, and it is not the one you expect. Understand the distinction first, because it is the whole hygiene in one stroke: I will sit through John Oliver castigating the man for a full segment; I will not mainline the castigated man himself. I do not watch Nick Fuentes. I do not listen to him, I do not clip-hunt him, I do not consume him ironically, and I want to be precise about why, because the reason is not that I am fragile and it is not that one viewing would convert me. I am a grown man with settled convictions, and I am also a sober man, and sober men learn exposure discipline. In high school I refused to read the Satanic Bible, not because I thought a chapter would flip me by Friday; I was a kid with the sense not to make a habit of the thing. That is the whole doctrine. A charming and funny and affable man, consumed as entertainment, even consumed as hate-watching, even consumed in quotation marks, is still exposure. It is like being exposed to drugs: it is not dangerous the first time, it is not dangerous the second time; the danger is chronic use. Sit with that voice often enough, laugh at the jokes often enough, let the cadence become furniture in your head, and before long your brain starts turning to tapioca. I do not need that in my life, and needing it less is the entire argument for not starting.

Understand: I am not a man without defenses. I have white blood cells. I have resistance built up over decades against Coast to Coast AM, which I have loved and handled safely since the Art Bell years; against Infowars, whose cadences I can hear coming a mile off; against James Corbett, who has been broadcasting his propaganda out of Japan for the last twenty years; against the No Agenda show, which I listen to with my antibodies up and my affection genuine. Those are fevers I have had and recovered from, and my system knows them. I have no such antibodies for Fuentes. Nobody my age does. He is a new pathogen of an old disease, and the honest response to a pathogen you have no immunity to is not to mainline it for research.

Because here is what the mockers keep missing, and it is the heart of this warning: the man is genuinely, frighteningly good at this. He has no college. He has nothing behind him—no institution, no network, no inheritance, no machine. And he walks into any room in that space and he is the smartest person in it, routinely, visibly smarter than Tucker Carlson, who has every credential and every resource and comes off dumb as rocks beside him. It is genius-level verbal fluency. He never ums. He never reaches. Every single word is perfectly elocuted and executed, hour after hour, and he is funny, and he is charming as hell, and charm is the one political asset you cannot buy, teach, or fact-check away. Worse—much worse—he is a true believer. He is not a shill. The forked tongue works because the man on the other end of it believes every word, and an audience can smell the difference between a performance and a conviction the way a dog smells weather. I say that as a man who is not a true believer in anything political; I'm only a true believer, and the totality of my belief belongs to God—which is exactly why I can assess this one coldly, without joining and without flinching. And let me put the superlative plainly, because dilution would be its own kind of lie: Nick Fuentes is uniquely dangerous. The combination—genius-level fluency without one wasted word, charm, youth with decades of runway, true belief instead of grift, total media-native mastery, and an announced strategy of collapse and fill the vacuum—does not repeat in any other figure in that space. There is no second one. I will give the superlative its full measure, and I would not say it if it weren't for real teeth and not just a forked tongue: I rank him the most dangerous figure in modern, post-war American history—above George W. Bush, above George H. W. Bush, above Donald Trump, above Ronald Reagan. Presidents are temporary tenants; they wield the system's power for a term, and then they leave, and the structure is still standing when they go. His project is the structure's collapse and replacement, pursued with a monk's discipline on a fifty-year horizon. That is a different category of danger, and it deserves to be named as one.

His Catholicism deserves one honest paragraph, because the cartoon version of it is another failure of observation. He is celibate, observant, right-wing Catholic, and there is nothing novel about the kind of Catholicism he represents; it is completely normalized in the traditionalist part of the Church that is forever getting threatened with schism and never quite splitting. The silent, non-political majority of Catholics in the pews—the ones who simply practice, confess, and believe the catechism means what it says—are far closer to that temper than to any hippie St. Francis. And since Francis has come up: the real Francis destroyed himself. He fasted his body to wreckage, slept in the cold, wept himself half blind, and after the stigmata at La Verna he spent the last two years of his life languishing on the cross, dead at about forty-four, spent on purpose. The garden statue is a domestication. The tradition Fuentes swims in has always had that hard, furnace edge, and pretending it was invented by a streamer is how comfortable people stay surprised.

Now, who is telling you all this, and why should the telling itself be trusted? Call it mashugana CYA if you like: a man delivering a warning like this one had better state exactly where he stands, in print, so the warning cannot be misread as sympathy. A Zionist, for one thing—my own conviction, nobody's brief: I believe in the right of Israel to exist, full stop. And I will go further, and I am not joking: if the world is going to be run by anybody, it should be run by the tribe of Israel, because they have an amazing playbook, and making the world a better place is literally in their source code—the Hebrew concept is Tikkun Olam, repairing the world. They tend to be a little interventionist, I grant you, affectionately and on the record, but their heart is in the right place. There is another line in that playbook that explains this entire essay: Lo Ta'amod—do not stand idly by. It is the Abrahamic Abraham in me, and I mean the first one, who stood in front of God and argued—argued!—for Sodom, a city that was not his, haggling the Almighty down from fifty righteous men to ten. And it comes from a Jesus freak, as I am, whose Jesus was a rabbi—the Gospels call him Rabbi to his face—and an observant one, who came not to abolish but to fulfill. I have come to think of him as a version controller: he wanted to restore the firmware, because the words had been used to build more and more solidity, more and more man and less and less God, until the scribes and Pharisees and Sadducees had become a golden calf for the one true God, and nobody remembered the original vision. Two kinds of observance, then. One restores the firmware and repairs the world. The other builds fences and goes hunting outside them. I know which kind I answer to, and it is why this essay exists. My rule for the split is the oldest one on record: I will give to Caesar what is Caesar's, and I will give to God what is God's. And I'm smart enough to know that Caesar is a role, not a regime. The church can be Caesar, and atheism can be Caesar, and humanism can be Caesar, and communism can be Caesar, and socialism can be Caesar, and capitalism can be Caesar, and Judaism can be Caesar, and Islam can be Caesar—anything at all that shows up demanding the share that belongs to God. Politics gets its due from me, and not one thing more.

And do not make the comfortable mistake about what he is selling, because he is not selling comfort. Fuentes is a warrior monk: celibate, ascetic, a true believer visibly willing to struggle, fight, and suffer for the thing; not a shill, not a grifter, not in it for the dollar bills. A cult of personality built on discipline and sacrifice is a different animal from one built on ease. The charm recruits—but the ascetic credibility is what makes followers trust him with everything, because a young man who is plainly paying costs himself can demand costs of them. His followers are not being entertained into this; they are being enlisted. He is more than the serpent in the garden with a good line. He is willing to stand as figurehead of a movement that would collapse the United States and rebuild something else in its place, and figureheads who believe are the kind that last.

I thought Charlie Kirk was amazing, and I will say the word plainly: I believe he is truly a martyr. I believe he had a good heart, and that he was trying to preach the gospel, and the proof of the heart was the discomfort—he said things that were not comfortable to just about everybody, in just about every situation; he was always stepping on toes, in every direction, including his own side's. He was not lying to people the way the churches, playing Caesar, so often do about actually needing to repent and sin no more. The church-as-Caesar has grown very good at selling all the good points and including none of the points of pain; Charlie Kirk refused to gild the lily. Fuentes is uniquely dangerous in exactly the way that Charlie Kirk wasn't, and mark this, because it is the tell: he wants his people to vote Democrat—not out of conversion, but arson—so that the Republican Party collapses, and into the vacuum left by the collapse he intends to pour his own vision. Kirk built inside the house. Fuentes is measuring the house for the fire and the empty lot after.

So, the forecast, and let me be clear that this is not a bit and not a wish. Free speech is free speech; I am not encouraging any of this; I am warning about it. If he is not Kirk'd—if nobody does to him what was done to Charlie—then time, talent, conviction, and charm compound in only one direction, and he becomes a major player in American politics. I have said it before and I will put it in print here: I believe he is a probable future president of the United States. The people who refuse to look at a rising force do not make it smaller. They only arrange their own surprise.

And understand that the forecast runs on both rails, because it has to; that is the thesis Oliver never touched. I hate to be the both sides guy, but I'm a bothsidist par excellence. In the same way, I think Alexandria Ocasio-Cortez is a future American president—young, media-native, genuinely believing, her pole's answer to him and his pole's best recruiting sergeant. But if you'll notice, they're both extremists. An AOC begets a Fuentes, and a Fuentes begets an AOC. Neither pole is first, neither pole is innocent; the circuit is closed, and a politics that only ever looks at one end of it will end up run by both ends of it.

Mark my words. Here's your warning, universe. Here's your warning, Earth. Here's your warning, North America. Here's your warning, America. Here's your warning, Washington, DC.
