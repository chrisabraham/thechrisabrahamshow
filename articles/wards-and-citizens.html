Wards and Citizens

An article by Chris Abraham, November 20, 2026

This article, read aloud:

Share: X · Facebook · LinkedIn · Reddit · Bluesky · Threads · Email

The modern state assigns wardship by category and never by capacity. Watch how the assignment happens. A clerk, a caseworker, a minister, a form: each one meets a person and reaches first for the file that names the person's class. The immigrant. The woman. The Black man, the brown man, the trans person, the gay man. The file arrives before the man does, and the file carries a standing instruction. This one is to be managed. This one is to be protected. This one speaks through an advocate, is housed through a program, is counted through a category. The citizen standing in the same office with the wrong file in his hand becomes a ward before he opens his mouth. A right exercised by a ward is an allowance with paperwork. A citizen's rights need no custodian, and the whole difference between the two conditions is who holds the key to the gate.

The classification comes first

Observe the order of operations in any government office in the Western world. The capacity of the individual in front of the desk is the last thing measured and usually goes unmeasured entirely. Nobody tests whether this woman runs a business, raises children, pays a mortgage, and settles her own disputes with her neighbors. Nobody tests whether this man has kept a family fed for thirty years. The category is read off the surface, the way a field guide reads plumage, and the handling follows the plumage. Whole populations receive the handling reserved for minors: supervision, representation by others, protection from their own choices, and a standing presumption that their judgment requires a countersignature.

The classification also runs in the other direction, and the notebook records that too. A person outside the protected categories who falls into trouble meets the state as an adult: fined, charged, taxed, and left to hire his own help. A person inside them who falls into the same trouble meets the state as a dependent: assessed, assigned, and accompanied. The two men can stand in the same queue with the same problem and be processed as different species of legal animal. One is a citizen in difficulty. The other is a ward in care. The difference sits in the category column of the form, and the category column is filled in before either man speaks.

Even if you were a freaking bear

The test of the system is the specimen it cannot explain. Put in front of the desk a man of complete and obvious capacity: large, competent, self-governing, dangerous if crossed, a man who has settled every account of his life with his own hands. In my own shorthand, even if you were a freaking bear. The classification holds anyway. The bear gets the ward's file if the bear's plumage matches a protected category, and he gets it without a single question about the bear. Size does not matter. Strength does not matter. A lifetime of demonstrated competence does not matter. The system was built to sort by class, and a sorting machine sorts what it was built to sort. Capacity is a fact about a person. Category is a fact about a filing system. The filing system wins every encounter, because the filing system holds the stamp.

That inversion is the tell. A state that assigned protection by capacity would send its caseworkers to the genuinely helpless: the child alone, the elder lost in dementia, the man whose mind has broken under him. A state that assigns protection by category builds an apparatus ten times the size of the need, staffed by people whose employment depends on the rolls staying full. It feeds. It expands its definition of the helpless. It finds new categories to file. The ward population grows at the speed of the budget that pays for finding wards.

Pity as policy

Pity is a private emotion with a decent record. A neighbor feels it, brings food, sits a while, and goes home. Pity becomes something else entirely when a legislature takes it up as an instrument. Written into policy, pity stops being a response to a person's condition and becomes a permanent description of a class of people. The pitied class is defined in statute as the class that cannot. Its members carry the definition into every office, every courtroom, and every negotiation of their lives. A landlord reads it. An employer reads it. A judge reads it. The policy meant to soften the world's treatment of the classified ends up instructing the world, in official language, that the classified are lesser animals in the legal habitat: handled gently, handled always, handled by someone else.

Watch what pity does to standing. A citizen makes a claim and the claim is heard as an assertion of right. A ward makes the same claim and it is heard as a request for consideration, routed to the people who speak for wards. The claim loses its force in the routing. Rights asserted in one's own voice carry the weight of the person asserting them. Rights requested through a custodian carry the weight the custodian assigns them, and custodians are cautious animals. They trade claims away for programs. They accept supervision as the price of assistance. They settle, because settling keeps the relationship with the state warm, and the relationship is the custodian's livelihood. The ward's own voice, raised in the ward's own name, is the one sound the whole apparatus is built to muffle.

Protection as custody

Every custody arrangement in ordinary life has a shape. A guardian is appointed for a specific person, for a specific incapacity, proven in a specific proceeding, and the appointment can be challenged and ended when the incapacity ends. Category wardship has none of that shape. It is appointed for millions at once, for an incapacity nobody proved, in no proceeding anyone attended, and it never sunsets. The protected person cannot petition out of the classification, because the classification is the lens through which his petition would be read. He can be a veteran, a proprietor, a grandfather, a bear. The file still says managed. The file still says protected. Protection on those terms is custody with better public relations, and the protected pay for it in the only currency that matters in a republic: standing.

The custodians benefit in a currency of their own. An entire professional class draws its salaries, its status, and its moral self-image from the existence of the wards. Advocates, administrators, compliance officers, diversity staff, case managers: the habitat is full of well-fed species whose niche is the helplessness of other people. Observe them without contempt, because the behavior is ordinary animal behavior. A species defends its food source. The food source here is the classification itself, so the classification is defended against every attempt to retire it, and the people filed under it are defended as a resource. The cruellest entry in the notebook is that the classified are often defended most fiercely at exactly the moments they assert the capacity that would free them from the file. A ward who speaks as a citizen is a threat to the ecosystem. A ward who performs helplessness is its harvest.

What a citizen is owed

The classified are owed more standing, and the debt is precise. A citizen is owed the presumption of capacity until incapacity is proven, person by person, in the open. A citizen is owed the right to be heard in his own voice, to hire his own advocate or fire one, to refuse help and accept the consequences of refusing. A citizen is owed the full risk of his own life, which is the same thing as the full dignity of it. The state's proper posture toward an adult is the posture of the zookeeper's opposite number in the wild: observe, keep the habitat lawful, intervene at the point of predation and injury, and otherwise leave the animals to their own society. Government that cannot leave adults alone has announced what it believes adults are. The announcement is the policy, whatever the brochure says.

None of this argues against help. Help offered to a citizen is a transaction between equals: he can take it, decline it, argue with its terms, and walk away from the table. Help assigned to a ward is a condition of his classification, and walking away from the table is itself recorded as a symptom. The same food bank, the same clinic, the same housing voucher is either a service or a leash depending on whether the man receiving it may refuse it without penalty and speak for himself without a translator from the custodial class. Keep the services. Retire the file. A republic can feed its people without first demoting them to children in its own records.

I stand inside this habitat too, and the notebook does not spare the observer. Every man is tempted to accept the ward's bargain when the bargain is offered to him: the protection is real, the paperwork is handled by others, and the price is charged to an account, standing, that never sends a statement. Whole classes have accepted it across generations and built identities inside the enclosure, and the gate, all this time, stands open in the fence of paper. The fence is paper. It was always paper. A citizen walks through paper. The first step is the whole argument: to insist on being met as a person of capacity, in one's own voice, under one's own name, and to let the classification starve for want of a specimen willing to stand still for the filing.
