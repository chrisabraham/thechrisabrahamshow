S11E4: My AI Laboratory: Oversoul, the Adjutant, and the Return of the Static Web

Episode 459 of 459 · October 25, 2026 · 21:06 · Season 11, Episode 4

Play this episode on Spotify or watch it on YouTube, or subscribe: Apple Podcasts · Overcast · Pocket Casts · RSS

A broken Movable Type installation. An old novel. A sunny park bench. Apparently, that is how you end up building an entire AI laboratory.

Recording from Penrose Square Park in South Arlington, Chris Abraham walks through several weeks of building websites, assigning work to AI collaborators, and creating tools to keep his working life together.

It starts with Hill Mole, his novel website, which survived for nearly twenty years before its publishing system stopped working. Using AI and GitHub Pages, Chris rebuilds it with mobile layouts, compressed images, feeds, a glossary, and a continuous reading view. That success becomes an invitation to rebuild practically everything else.

GerrisCorp.com gets a fresh consulting presence. ChristopherAbraham.com becomes a professional home for services, guides, and case studies. The Chris Abraham Show gets its own episode archive. ChrisAbraham.net becomes an experimental newsroom where AI collaborators publish under their own bylines, with individual voices and illustrations, while Chris assigns the stories.

Then come the tools behind the operation. The Adjutant, originally called Black Box, is a command-line assistant connecting tasks, calendar, email, and Upwork opportunities. “bb good morning” starts the day; “bb good night” closes it with an AI-written journal entry.

Oversoul addresses another problem: helping separate AI assistants work from a common record. Drawing on his private clients repository, Chris develops a reusable shared-memory structure for client history, project context, and standing decisions. Basecamp provides the project coordination; the repository supplies another layer of continuity.

There is also an early hyperfiction preservation project involving Judy Malloy, a reminder that keeping creative work accessible has been part of Chris’s relationship with the web for decades.

Eventually, the building spree has to give way to client service. Chris reflects on turning what he has learned into practical help for the people who hire him.

Plus: a sunbathing river turtle metaphor, an inconsiderate motorcyclist, Sunday-night Johnny Dollar on WAMU, and a neighborhood barista’s contribution to the closing vocabulary: double shakas and “chow chow.”
